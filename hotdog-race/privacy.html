Privacy Policy

Hotdog Race keeps an account so your name, your hotdog and everything you own follow you between devices. This page says exactly what that account holds, who can see it, and how to make it go away.

Effective date: 29 August 2026

The English text of this policy is the authoritative version; the Hungarian and Pirate English versions are provided for convenience.

The short version: you can play Practice without an account and without an internet connection, and nothing leaves your device. Online racing needs an account, and an account means an e-mail address or a linked sign-in, a display name, your hotdog's colours, your balances and your race results. There is no advertising, no analytics SDK and no cross-app tracking anywhere in the game.

1. Who is responsible

Vitya Games (“we”) develops and operates Hotdog Race and the account and matchmaking service behind it, and is the controller of the personal data described here. You can reach us at the address in the Contact section at the bottom of this page.

2. Playing without an account

Practice mode runs entirely on your device against nine bots. It needs no account, no sign-in and no network connection, it grants no rewards, and it sends us nothing at all. Nothing signs you in on its own: an account exists only because somebody registered one.

3. What stays on your device

  • A local cache of your display name, colours and balances, so a screen can be drawn before the server answers. Nothing in this file grants a balance or an item — the server’s answer is the only one that counts.
  • Your session token, which acts as your sign-in until it expires.
  • Your keyboard bindings and settings.

Deleting the app, or clearing its local data, removes all of it from the device. It does not delete the account itself — see section 11.

4. What the account service stores

Data Why it exists
An internal account identifier and the date it was registered To be the one account your sign-ins, your progress and your purchases all attach to
Your e-mail address, and a scrypt hash of your password — never the password itself To let you sign in with an e-mail and password on any platform
For each linked sign-in, the provider’s name and the stable identifier it issues for you To recognise you when you sign in with Google or a platform account
Your display name and your hotdog’s three colour choices and equipped accessory To show your racer to you and to the other nine racers in a race
Your balances, your lifetime podium points, the items you own and an audit record of every balance change To keep what you earned and bought, and to be able to answer honestly when a balance looks wrong
A one-way hash of each active session token To keep you signed in, and to be able to revoke that sign-in immediately
Your seat in each race you were admitted to, and your result: finishing place, time and points To pay the reward for that race exactly once, and to keep a race that already happened true for everyone in it
The storefront transaction identifier behind each verified purchase To grant a purchase once and only once, and to be able to look one up if you ask about it

Your network address is processed by the service that terminates the connection, so that a request can be answered and so that sign-in attempts can be rate-limited. Server logs record which race you were placed in, how many humans and bots were in it and how the process ended. Session tokens, admission tickets and passwords are never written to a log.

5. Signing in

E-mail and password is always offered, because it is the one sign-in that belongs to no platform and therefore works on all of them. Passwords are stored only as a scrypt hash and compared in constant time; a wrong password and an unknown address are refused with the same sentence, so nobody can use the sign-in form to find out who has an account here.

Google Sign-In opens your system browser on Google’s own account chooser and asks for the openid, email and profile scopes. Of what Google returns we store one thing: the stable identifier that tells us this is the same Google account next time. Your Google profile name is offered only as a starting display name, which you can change and which a later sign-in never overwrites. We do not store your Google e-mail address, your Google profile picture, your contacts or anything else from your Google Account, and we do not use Google data for advertising or share it with anyone.

Platform sign-ins — Game Center on Apple devices, and other storefront identities as they are added — work the same way: the platform proves who you are, we keep the identifier it issues, and the platform never carries any part of a race. Adding a second sign-in while you are already signed in links it to the account you already have instead of creating another one; an identity that already belongs to a different account is refused rather than silently merged.

6. Purchases

Where the game offers paid credits, the payment itself is handled entirely by the storefront you bought from — Apple, Google or another platform. We never see your card number, your billing address or your payment method. What reaches us is the storefront’s signed confirmation that a transaction happened; we verify it, grant the credits once, and keep the transaction identifier so the same purchase can never be granted twice or attached to somebody else’s account.

7. What Hotdog Race never does

  • It contains no advertising and no advertising SDK.
  • It contains no third-party analytics or attribution SDK.
  • It does not track you across other apps or websites, and does not build a profile of you.
  • It does not access your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • It has no chat, no voice and no way to upload content, so there is no user-generated content to moderate or store.
  • We do not sell or rent personal data, and we do not share it for anyone else’s marketing.

8. Why we are allowed to process it

  • Performance of a contract: running your account, seating you in races, keeping your progress and completing your purchases.
  • Legitimate interests: keeping the service available and the races fair — rate limiting, lockout after repeated wrong passwords, and the audit trail behind every balance change and administrative action.
  • Legal obligation: keeping the records that tax and consumer law require us to keep about a purchase.

9. Who else can see it

  • Other racers see, during a race and on its result screen, exactly what a race needs: your display name and your hotdog’s appearance. They do not see your e-mail address, your balances or your purchases.
  • Our hosting provider, which runs the servers the account service and the race processes live on.
  • The storefront and identity providers you chose to use — Apple, Google — under their own privacy policies.
  • Authorities, where the law actually requires it.

10. How long we keep it

  • Account, profile, wallet, inventory and race results: for as long as the account exists.
  • Sessions: they expire on their own, and a password change or a suspension revokes every live one immediately.
  • Purchase records: for as long as accounting and consumer-law obligations require.
  • Server logs: kept briefly, for operating and securing the service.

11. Your rights, and deleting your account

You can ask for a copy of your data, correct it, have it deleted, restrict or object to its processing, and receive it in a portable form. Your display name and your hotdog’s appearance you can correct yourself, in the game, at any time.

Deleting your account removes your profile, every linked sign-in and credential, your sessions, your wallet and its ledger, your inventory, and your own race results. Two things deliberately survive: an administrative record that a deletion happened and why, kept without your account identifier; and the other nine racers’ results from races you took part in, because their race is not yours to take with you. The full procedure is on the account deletion page.

If you are in the European Economic Area and you think we have handled your data badly, you can complain to your national data protection authority.

12. Children

Hotdog Race is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly create accounts for them. If you believe a child has registered an account, write to us and we will delete it.

13. Changes to this policy

If the game’s data practices change, this page and the store privacy disclosures are updated before the changed version is released, and the effective date above changes with them.
