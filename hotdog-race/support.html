How a race works

  • Press RACE. You join a queue that closes after at most ten seconds.
  • Every race has exactly ten seats. Any seat still open when the queue closes is taken by a bot, so a race never fails to start.
  • A three-second countdown holds everybody at the start gate, then the course opens.
  • Falling into a pit or being hit by a gull is a knockout followed by a three-second respawn at the starting grid — you are not out of the race.
  • An official race ends after three minutes whether or not everybody has finished. Practice has no clock at all and shows FREE instead.

Controls

Five inputs, the same five for a player and for a bot: move left, move right, jump, crouch or slide, and use.

  • On a touch screen: the on-screen stick moves, and the buttons beside it jump, crouch and use.
  • On a keyboard, out of the box: arrow keys to move, Up to jump, Down to crouch, Space to use, and Escape or R to leave a race.
  • Every key can be rebound in Settings → Controls. Bindings follow the key’s position on the keyboard, so WASD stays a square on any layout.

Reading the course

  • Rotating sweepers are a timing problem: watch one full turn before you commit.
  • A low gate can only be passed crouching or sliding.
  • A shuttle tunnel closes and sweeps back toward its entrance. If it catches you inside, stay low and move left before trying again.
  • Gulls cross the whole course from the finish side. A low one has to be jumped and a middle-lane one has to be ducked. They never strike at the starting grid.
  • Jump pads only work if you jump on them: standing or running across one does nothing.

My phone and my PC pressed RACE and never met

The platform is deliberately not part of matchmaking, so a phone and a PC belong in the same queue. What does separate two players is the build they are running: a client speaking an older protocol or content version cannot be placed in a race with a newer one, because they would not agree about the course. The matchmaking screen says so when it happens. Update both copies to the same version and try again.

Accounts and sign-in

  • RACE and CO-OP need an account; PRACTICE does not, and it works with no connection at all.
  • An e-mail address and a password of at least 10 characters works on every platform. Google Sign-In opens your system browser. Game Center is offered on Apple devices.
  • To use one account everywhere, sign in first and then add the second sign-in from the account screen. That links it to the account you already have instead of starting a second one.
  • Ten wrong passwords lock the account for fifteen minutes. Waiting is the fix.
  • There is no self-service password reset yet. If you cannot get back in, write to us from the address on the account and we will sort it out.
  • Your name is yours. Signing in never overwrites it with the name your identity provider knows you by.

Credits, the shop and purchases

  • Finishing an official race pays earned credits, and the podium adds lifetime points. Practice pays nothing.
  • Sausage, bun and condiment colours are free. Accessories are bought in the shop with earned or premium credits and stay on the account.
  • A balance that looks wrong is worth writing to us about: every movement is recorded, so we can tell you what happened.
  • Refunds are handled by the storefront you bought from, not by us.

Troubleshooting

  • If a race never starts, check that you have a working internet connection: online racing needs one, Practice does not.
  • If a race stutters, the connection is the usual cause. Your own racer is predicted locally, so the other nine are the first to show it.
  • If there is no sound, check the device volume and the silent-mode switch.
  • If the game stops responding, close it and open it again. Your account lives on the server, so nothing you own is lost.
  • The version line on the home screen has three numbers on it. Quote all three when you report a problem.
