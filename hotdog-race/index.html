Hotdog Race

Ten hotdogs. One finish line. Nine of them are not your friends.

Press RACE and you are in a queue that seals after ten seconds. Every race seats exactly ten, whichever device the other nine came from — and any seat still open at the bell is filled by a bot, so the start never waits for you.

Three ways to run

Race it, share it, or practise it.

RACE

The main event. Ten seats, one cross-play queue, and a finish order the server decides. First place pays the most, the podium collects lifetime points, and everybody who starts gets paid something.

10 players · account required

CO-OP

Two racers, one kitchen, and a course neither of you finishes alone. Swing ropes, two-lever doors, sticky sauce and a shopping cart built for two — the run only counts when both of you are through.

2 players · finish together

PRACTICE

The whole course list, offline, against nine bots, with no account and no clock. It pays nothing, which is exactly why it is open to everybody the moment the game starts.

1 player · no account

Your dog, your table

Everything you own follows the account, not the device.

Sign in with an e-mail address, with Google, or with the platform you are already on. Whichever you pick, it is the same hotdog, the same wallet and the same shelf of accessories on the next screen you open.

Built around fair play

The server is the referee.

Every official race is simulated by a dedicated server that has no player of its own. There is no host, so there is no host advantage: the finish order, the respawn timers, the knockouts and the rewards are all decided in one place, and a client cannot send a result it did not earn.
